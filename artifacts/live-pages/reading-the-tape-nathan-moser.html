For the next call

  1. Break the demo into checkpoints. Instead of one continuous walkthrough, pause every 60–90 seconds with a direct question — turns a 3:38 monologue into a conversation and surfaces objections while there's still time on the clock to handle them.
  2. Slow down specifically for numbers. Your general pace is fine; the pricing recap is where 189 wpm works against you. A deliberate half-beat before each dollar figure would make the $9,110 number land clearer the first time.
  3. Reclaim eye contact during the first 60–90 seconds. You're fully present once rapport-building starts — worth carrying that into the very opening, since that's the one stretch where your attention visibly reads as elsewhere.
  4. Nothing to fix on vocal energy or expressiveness — the pitch and thumbs-up-at-9:05 data both point to genuine, well-modulated delivery. Don't over-correct here.

Methodology & limits

Read this before treating any finding above as more certain than it is

  • Two calls were pulled from Apollo Conversations for this pilot. Bernadette Anderson's (Aug 26) was excluded entirely — it was dialed as a phone call through Zoom Phone, and its "video" recording turned out to be a screen capture of the dialer app, not a camera feed. There was nothing to look at. This one, with Nathan, was chosen instead because it's an actual Google Meet recording with your camera on.
  • Nathan's camera was off for the entire call — every frame just shows a "NPM Ventures" name card. Across both calls checked for this pilot, the client's camera was never once on. That's a small sample, but it's worth planning around: video-based coaching on this system will likely only ever be able to say something about you, not reliably about the person you're talking to.
  • Facial/eye-contact review here was a human-style visual read of sampled frames (roughly one every 30–90 seconds, plus some chosen around key moments), not an automated emotion-recognition or gaze-tracking model. No such model was used anywhere in this report — that category of tool is unreliable enough that treating its output as fact would be worse than not measuring at all.
  • Pitch and energy numbers are computed (via pitch-tracking and loudness analysis on your isolated speaking segments, using the call's own per-speaker timestamps), not a human listening — and pitch-tracking algorithms occasionally jump an octave on quiet or noisy audio, which can inflate variability numbers. The ranges reported (percentiles, not raw averages) are the more robust reading.
  • Talk-time, pace, and the 3:38 figure come directly from Apollo's own per-speaker transcript timestamps — these are the most reliable numbers in this report.
  • This was a one-call pilot, at your request, kept as a standalone page rather than added to Nathan's contact brief. Nothing here updates automatically and nothing runs again unless you ask for it.
